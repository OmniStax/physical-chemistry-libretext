The van der Waals equation as a virial series

The van der Waals equation can be written as:

a and b from molecular parameters

Using a combination of the hard sphere model and the Lennard-Jones potential, we obtain:

$$ \ku(\kr) = \begin{cases} \infty & \kr \leq \ksigmahs \\ -\dfrac{C_6}{\kr^6} & \kr > \ksigmahs \end{cases} \tag{16.7.1} $$

We can write the second Virial coefficient as

$$ \kBtwo(\kT) = \dfrac{2}{3} \pi N_0 \ksigmahs^3 \left[ 1 - \dfrac{C_6}{3 k_B \kT \ksigmahs^6} \right] \tag{16.7.2} $$

Let us introduce to simplifying variables

$$ \begin{aligned} \kbvdw &= \dfrac{2}{3} \pi N_0 \ksigmahs^3 \\ a &= \dfrac{2 \pi N_0^2 C_6}{9 \ksigmahs^3} \end{aligned} \tag{16.7.3} $$

In which the second Virial coefficient becomes:

$$ \kBtwo(\kT) = \kbvdw - \dfrac{a}{R\kT} \tag{16.7.4} $$

From these equations, we can see that $a$ is directly proportional to $c_6$, the intermolecular attraction between particles with a force proportional to $\kr^{-6}$. Likewise, $\kbvdw$ is equivalent to four times the volume of the particle. In other words, the van der Waals equations assumes the hard sphere model at small distances and weak interactions (attractive forces) at larger distances.
