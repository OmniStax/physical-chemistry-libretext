# The Properties of Gases

In this chapter, we look at the properties of real gases to see how interactions between molecules cause deviations from the ideal gas law. As the simplest equation of state, the ideal gas law is often used to get a good approximation for the pressure, density, and temperature for a gaseous sample, and at high temperatures and low pressures, it is an very good approximation, A “perfect” gas consists of noninteracting particles that individually have no volume: there are no collisions between gas particles because they take up no space. However, as the gas density/pressure increases, or as the temperature decreases, deviations from ideal behavior are observed– atoms and molecules do experience attractive and repulsive forces.
