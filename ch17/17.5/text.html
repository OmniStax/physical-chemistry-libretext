Gas Compressed by a Piston

Let us consider a simple thought experiment, which is illustrated in the figure above: A system of $N$ particles is compressed by a piston pushing in the positive $\kz$ direction. Since this is a classical thought experiment, we think in terms of forces. The piston exerts a constant force of magnitude $\kF$ on the system. The direction of the force is purely in the positive $\kz$ direction, so that we can write the force vector $\bf{F}$ as $\bf{F} = \begin{pmatrix} 0, 0, F \end{pmatrix}$. At equilibrium (the piston is not moving), the system exerts an equal and opposite force on the piston of the form $\begin{pmatrix} 0, 0, -F \end{pmatrix}$. If the energy of the system is $\kE$, then the force exerted by the system on the piston will be given by the negative change in $\kE$ with respect to $\kz$:

$$ -\kF = -\dfrac{d\kE}{d\kz} \tag{17.5.1} $$

or:

$$ \kF = \dfrac{d\kE}{d\kz} \tag{17.5.2} $$

The force exerted by the system on the piston is manifest as an observable pressure $\kP$ equal to the force $\kF$ divided by the area $A$ of the piston, $\kP=\kF/A$. Given this, the observed pressure is just:

$$ \kP = \dfrac{d\kE}{Ad\kz} \tag{17.5.3} $$

Since the volume decreases when the system is compressed, we see that $Ad\kz = -d\kV$. Hence, we can write the pressure as $\kP = -d\kE/d\kV$.

Of course, the relation $\kP = -d\kE/d\kV$ is a thermodynamic one, but we need a function of $x$ that we can average over the ensemble. The most natural choice is:

$$ p(x) = -\dfrac{d \kEcal (x)}{d\kV} \tag{17.5.4} $$

so that $\kP = \langle p(x) \rangle$. Setting up the average, we obtain:

$$ \begin{aligned} \kP &= -\dfrac{C_N}{\kQsys(N, \kV, \kT)} \int \dfrac{\partial \kEcal}{\partial \kV} e^{-\beta \kEcal (x)} \\ &= \dfrac{C_N}{\kQsys(N, \kV, \kT)} \dfrac{1}{\beta} \int \dfrac{\partial}{\partial \kV} e^{-\beta \kEcal (x)} \\ &= \dfrac{k\kT}{\kQsys(N, \kV, \kT} \dfrac{\partial}{\partial \kV} C_N \int e^{-\beta \kEcal (x)} \\ &= k\kT \left( \dfrac{\partial \: \text{ln} \: \kQsys(N, \kV, \kT)}{\partial \kV} \right) \end{aligned} \tag{17.5.5} $$

Ideal Gas in the Canonical Ensemble

Recall that the mechanical energy for an ideal gas is:

$$ \kEcal (x) = \sum_{i=1}^N \dfrac{\kpivec^2}{2\km} \tag{17.5.6} $$

where all particles are identical and have mass $\km$. Thus, the expression for the canonical partition function $\kQsys(N, \kV, \kT)$ is:

$$ \kQsys(N, \kV, \kT) = \dfrac{1}{N!h^{3N}} \int dx \: e^{-\beta \sum_{i=1}^N \kpivec^2/2\km} $$
