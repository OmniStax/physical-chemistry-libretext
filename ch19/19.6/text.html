Path B + C in small steps

We can make the same argument for the heat along C. If we do the three processes A and B+C only to a tiny extent we can write:

$$ \delta \kw_B = -\delta \kq_C \quad\Rightarrow\quad -\dfrac{\knmol \kR \kT}{\kV}\,d\kV = \kCv\,d\kT $$

And now we can integrate from $\kVa$ to $\kVb$ over the reversible adiabatic work along B and from $\kTone$ to $\kTtwo$ for the reversible isochoric heat along C. To separate the variables we do need to bring the temperature to the right side of the equation.:

$$ \dfrac{3}{2}\knmol \kR \int_{\kTone}^{\kTtwo} \dfrac{d\kT}{\kT} = -\knmol \kR \int_{\kVa}^{\kVb} \dfrac{d\kV}{\kV} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \dfrac{\kTtwo}{\kTone} = \left(\dfrac{\kVa}{\kVb}\right)^{2/3} $$

The latter expression is valid for a reversible adiabatic expansion of a monatomic ideal gas (say Argon) because we used the $\kCv$ expression for such a system. We can use the gas law $\kP\kV=\knmol \kR\kT$ to translate this expression in one that relates pressure and volume see Eq 19.23
