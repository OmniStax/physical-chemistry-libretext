The combined first and second laws

Modeling the dependence of the Gibbs and Helmholtz functions behave with varying temperature, pressure, and volume is fundamentally useful. But in order to do that, a little bit more development is necessary. To see the power and utility of these functions, it is useful to combine the First and Second Laws into a single mathematical statement. In order to do that, one notes that since

$$ d\kSent = \dfrac{d\kq}{\kT} $$

for a reversible change, it follows that

$$ d\kq= \kT d\kSent $$

And since

$$ d\kw = \kT d\kSent - \kp d\kV $$

for a reversible expansion in which only p-V works is done, it also follows that (since $d\kU=d\kq+d\kw$):

$$ d\kU = \kT d\kSent - \kp d\kV \tag{22.3.1} $$

This is an extraordinarily powerful result. This differential for $d\kU$ can be used to simplify the differentials for $\kHenth$, $\kA$, and $\kG$. But even more useful are the constraints it places on the variables $\kT$, $\kSent$, $\kp$, and $\kV$ due to the mathematics of exact differentials!

Maxwell Relations

The above result suggests that the natural variables of internal energy are $\kSent$ and $\kV$ (or the function can be considered as $\kU(\kSent, \kV)$). So the total differential ($d\kU$) in Equation 22.3.1 can be expressed:

This is an example of a Maxwell Relation. These are very powerful relationship that allows one to substitute partial derivatives when one is more convenient (perhaps it can be expressed entirely in terms of $\alpha$ and/or $\kappa_T$ for example.)

A similar result can be derived based on the definition of $\kHenth$.

This is the Maxwell relation on $\kHenth$. Maxwell relations can also be developed based on $\kA$ and $\kG$. The results of those derivations are summarized in Table 22.3.1.

Table 22.3.1Maxwell Relations

$\kU$$d\kU = \kT d\kSent - \kp d\kV$$\kSent, \,\kV$$\left( \dfrac{\partial \kT}{\partial \kV} \right)_{\kSent} = - \left( \dfrac{\partial \kp}{\partial \kSent} \right)_{\kV}$
$\kHenth$$d\kHenth = \kT d\kSent + \kV d\kp$$\kSent, \,\kp$$\left( \dfrac{\partial \kT}{\partial \kp} \right)_{\kSent} = \left( \dfrac{\partial \kV}{\partial \kSent} \right)_{\kp}$

$\kG$$d\kG = \kV d\kp - \kSent d\kT$$\kp, \,\kT$$\left( \dfrac{\partial \kV}{\partial \kT} \right)_{\kp} = - \left( \dfrac{\partial \kSent}{\partial \kp} \right)_{\kT}$

The Maxwell relations are extraordinarily useful in deriving the dependence of thermodynamic variables on the state variables of $\kp$, $\kT$, and $\kV$.
