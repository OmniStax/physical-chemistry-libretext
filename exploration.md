# Exploration: Physical Chemistry (LibreTexts), the full-book pass

Written 2026-11-08, before any section was built (root rules 1, 18 and 23).
The source of record is the LibreTexts site itself, page 9054
"Physical Chemistry (LibreTexts)" on chem.libretexts.org, mirrored into
`source/` (gitignored) by `tools/fetch.py` through the site's public
MindTouch API: the page tree, every page's body as rendered for viewing, and
every page's tags. No PDF was downloaded; the site's own pages are the book.

What was read: every page was fetched (379) and converted to markdown with
`tools/lt2md.py`; every page of Chapter 1 was read in full; for the other
chapters the converted pages were read at their headings, boxes, figures,
tables and equation markers, and the counts below are taken from those
conversions. Chapters 2 to 33 still have to be read in full by their own
chapter explorations.

## What the book is

A LibreTexts **Textbook Map**: an open book whose chapter plan follows a
commercial text (McQuarrie and Simon, *Physical Chemistry: A Molecular
Approach*) while its pages are assembled from many open sources. The page
credits name, among others, Michael Fowler (UVA), Hanson, Harvey, Sweeney and
Zielinski (*Quantum States of Atoms and Molecules*), OpenStax, Kyle Crabtree,
Mark Tuckerman, Claire Vallance, Jerry LaRue, Stefan Franzen, Tom Neils and
Howard DeVoe's *Thermodynamics and Chemistry*; the curator is Delmar Larsen.
This shapes everything else in this file:

- **The voice changes from page to page**, and so do the notation and the
  apparatus. Chapter 1 alone mixes Fowler's lectures, OpenStax prose and
  LibreTexts editorial notes.
- **The book is uneven.** Chapters 1 to 10 carry learning objectives,
  worked examples and inline exercises; the later chapters are mostly
  derivation with little apparatus (Chapter 14 has no example and no
  exercise; Chapters 17 to 31 have almost no exercises).
- **Typos and slips are frequent** (Chapter 1: "Stefan-Boltzman", "relavant",
  "fluoresce light tube", "$e = 1.60217662 \times 10^{-19}\,C^2$"). Root rule 13's
  correction mechanism will be used more often than in an OpenStax book.

## The organising units

book → chapters (33) → sections (numbered N.M, one page each) → `<h2>`
headers, sometimes `<h3>` → boxes (objectives, examples, exercises, notes).

| Sections N.M | 288, plus 2 sub-pages (32.10.1, 32.10.2) |
| Chapter summary pages N.S (Chapters 3 to 10, 12 to 17) | 14 |
| Chapter exercise pages N.E | 27 |
| Reference-table pages 12.T (Character Tables, Correlation Tables) | 2 |
| Back matter: Index, Glossary, Detailed Licensing | 3 |

The mapping to OmniStax needs no folding: one section page, one OmniStax
page. A chapter's landing page (an epigraph or a short paragraph, and a
"Thumbnail" credit) is the chapter's `intro/` page; an N.S page is the
chapter's `summary/` page (root rule 21). The front and back matter are not
pages: the Index and Glossary are generated by the site (the Glossary page
body is empty in the API) and the licensing pages are rendered by script.

Chapters 32 (Math Chapters: complex numbers, probability, partial
differentiation, series, vectors, matrices, determinants, Fourier analysis,
operators …) and 33 (Appendices: deriving Planck's distribution law, bra-ket
notation) are numbered like chapters and built like chapters, at the end.

`modules.json` records every page with its LibreTexts id, path and licence;
`toc.md` lists them for people.

## Where the apparatus sits

Everything is inside the section page. In order:

- **Learning objectives**: a `section.box-objectives` at the top, a lead-in
  sentence and a list. 76 of 290 sections, nearly all in Chapters 1 to 10.
  The lead-in sentence is often the section's own opening ("One experimental
  phenomenon that could not be adequately explained by classical physics was
  blackbody radiation. Objectives for this section include"); it is an
  objective's lead-in, not prose, and goes to the tables with the list.
- **The narrative**: `<h2>` headers (the book's own sub-concept headers, kept
  per root rule 3 where they apply), prose with `<strong>` for a term it
  defines (but also for plain emphasis: "{term:will} radiate"), MathJax TeX
  (`\( \)` inline, `\[ \]` display, `\label`/`\ref`), figures with
  `<figcaption>` "Figure N.M.K: …", tables with a `<caption>` "Table N.M.K: …",
  boxes and the occasional embedded video or applet (4).
- **Worked examples**: `section.box-example`, titled "Example N.M.K: Title"
  (the title is optional), the problem, then `<h6>` parts: "Solution",
  "Solution A/B/C", "Strategy". Numbered per section by LibreTexts'
  `\PageIndex{K}`.
- **Inline exercises**: `section.box-exercise`, "Exercise N.M.K: Title",
  usually right after the example it parallels. The answer, where there is
  one, is a collapsible `<dl>` with `<dt>Answer</dt>` (or "Answer a",
  "Solution"); 79 of 151 carry one. An unkeyed box is often a "show that"
  derivation (1.2.1).
- **Notes**: `section.box-note` and `box-emphasis` (196 over the book): titled
  asides ("Not all radiators are blackbody radiators", "Max Planck
  (1858–1947)", "Humor: Heisenberg and the Police"), and `box-definition`
  in a few later chapters.
- **Summaries**: a "## Summary" header closing 19 sections (to `summary_html`),
  and the chapter summary pages N.S of Chapters 3–10 and 12–17 (to the
  chapter's `summary/` page).
- **Problem lists**: a few sections end on a heading of numbered questions:
  "Conceptual Questions" (1.3, five, unkeyed), "Problems" in Chapters 12, 17,
  18 and 27. Unkeyed throughout.
- **Contributors and Attributions**: the closing list of every page, the
  page's credits. It is attribution, not prose: it goes to the section's
  attribution (`book.json` credits and the section's `notes`), never into
  `text.html`.

### The exercise pages N.E

All but one are empty or stubs. Of the 27 N.E pages, 24 have no body at
all; 19.E holds a stray copy of a Chapter 4 passage on the uncertainty
principle with one exercise box; 21.E holds a single "Problem 1" (an
entropy change of cooling water), unkeyed; 15.E, 24.E and 30.E hold empty
markup. The book's exercises are therefore the inline boxes and the
problem lists; the chapter pages are not built, and 21.E's problem goes to
the section of Chapter 21 that teaches what it tests.

### The answer key

There is no separate key. An inline exercise is keyed when its box carries
an Answer part (79 of 151); worked examples carry their solutions (109 of
161 have a titled Solution part; most of the rest work the solution inline,
1.3.1 after a "Strategy"). The problem lists are unkeyed. Several keyed
answers are explanations rather than results (1.3.2, 1.6.2) and several are
long worked computations (1.8.1, 1.8.2): they are the book's and are kept
as printed.

## Licence and attribution

The book is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (tag on page 9054), but LibreTexts licenses
each page: 300 pages CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, 36 CC BY 4.0, 10 CC BY-SA 4.0, 33
untagged (landing pages and front and back matter, which inherit the
book's). A page adapted from a CC BY-SA page has to stay **CC BY-SA**, since
BY-SA does not allow a non-commercial clause to be added; a CC BY page may be
shared as BY-NC-SA. The section therefore records its own licence, and the
footer prints it. The figure credits are a parenthetical at the end of the
caption, "(CC BY-NC; Ümit Kaya via LibreTexts)", and a kept image keeps it.
The section's Contributors and Attributions list is its byline.

## The source format, and what the converter does

`tools/lt2md.py` turns one page's view HTML into markdown with the markers
its docstring lists (`{eq:label}`, `[ref:eq:label]`, `{term:…}`,
`> FIGURE {fig:N.M.K}`, `> IMAGE`, `> TABLE`, `> EMBED`, `:::example`,
`:::exercise`, `:::note [kind]`, `[text](page:<id>)`), and
`tools/convert.py N` writes Chapter N's pages to `chNN/<page>/source.md`.
Things the conversion has to know about:

- `\PageIndex{K}` is how LibreTexts numbers boxes, figures and tables per
  page; the converter writes N.M.K. A figure's number in the prose is the
  same text, so a citation is "Figure 1.1.2".
- **Equation numbers are MathJax's**, `tags: "all"`, prefixed by the page
  number: every display without `\nonumber` is numbered, and an `align`
  numbers every row. The `n=` the converter writes after a marker is its
  own count of displays and is a first approximation only; the chapter
  exploration checks the numbers the prose cites against the live page.
- **TeX macros beyond KaTeX's**: `\op`, `\ket`, `\bra`, `\braket` (the
  site's MathJax configuration), `\ce` (mhchem, 424 uses), `\cancel` and
  `\cancelto`, and page-local `\newcommand` blocks (110), chiefly in the
  thermodynamics chapters taken from DeVoe (`\Del`, `\Delsub`, `\st`, `\tx`,
  `\units` …). The book's macro table (KaTeX macros in `book.json`, or
  expansion in the converter) is decided in the chapter that first needs
  each; Chapter 1 needs only `\ce`, `\cancel` and `\dfrac`.
- Bold is overloaded (term and emphasis); `{term:…}` marks bold and the
  builder decides which bolds are terms.
- Captions sometimes sit on an outer `<figure>` around several inner ones
  (Figure 1.4.1, three panels): one figure, several originals.
- Animated GIFs (Figures 1.8.6, 1.9.2) and embedded videos (1.9) occur.
- Images come from `@api/deki/files/<id>/<name>`, served through
  files.mtstatic.com; `tools/fetch.py --media N` downloads a chapter's images
  into `source/media/<id>-<name>`.

## Types (a first proposal)

The book is physical chemistry from quantum mechanics to kinetics, so the
categories (root rule 7) are many. Proposed in scheme order; each chapter
adds what it binds:

energy (with potential energy, kinetic energy, the work function and the
energy levels E_n as variants), temperature, wavelength, frequency,
wavenumber, momentum, velocity, length (position, radius, box length),
time, mass, charge, potential (stopping voltage), power (radiated power,
spectral energy density as its own type?), wavefunction, probability
density, angular momentum, operator, pressure, volume, entropy, heat,
work, heat capacity, enthalpy, Gibbs energy, chemical potential, partition
function, amount of substance, concentration, equilibrium constant, rate,
rate constant.

Untyped and in ink: constants of nature (h, ħ, c, k_B, σ, R_H), quantum
numbers and other counts, ratios and labels. Whether energy and its
variants are one type or several, and whether wavenumber is its own type or
a variant of frequency, is settled in `COLOR.md` with the user.

## Where the book is the most interesting to see, and what would make it stunning (root rule 23)

Physical chemistry is the subject where the reader most needs to *see* a
function change, and the printed book shows a few frozen curves. The
OmniStax edition can make each chapter's central function a thing the
reader drives:

- **One spectrum, from the forge to the sun** (1.1–1.2): a blackbody whose
  temperature slider moves the curve, the peak (Wien), the area (Stefan–
  Boltzmann) and the body's colour in its real colour, with the Rayleigh–
  Jeans curve diverging beside Planck's. The ultraviolet catastrophe as
  something the reader watches happen.
- **A photoelectric bench** (1.3): frequency and intensity sliders on a
  lamp over a metal; electrons leave or do not, the stopping voltage is
  read off, and the KE-versus-ν line builds as the reader takes data.
- **The hydrogen ladder** (1.4–1.8): levels E_n, transitions dropping as
  photons in their real colour, the Lyman, Balmer and Paschen series lit
  on a spectrum strip, the Bohr orbits beside them.
- **Waves and boxes** (2–3): a string whose normal modes add up live; the
  particle in a box with ψ and |ψ|² for any n, a 2D and 3D box, degeneracy
  appearing as the box becomes a cube.
- **Oscillators and rotors** (5): the harmonic and Morse potentials with
  their levels and wavefunctions, tunnelling into the forbidden region;
  a rigid rotor's rotational spectrum.
- **Orbitals in 3D** (6, 8–10): hydrogen orbitals as surfaces and clouds
  the reader turns, radial distributions, hybrid orbitals, MO diagrams that
  morph as atoms approach.
- **Symmetry you can do** (12): a molecule in 3D, a symmetry operation
  applied by a button, the character table row lighting up.
- **Spectra from levels** (13–15): rovibrational bands built from the
  levels and populations, the laser's population inversion.
- **The bridge from levels to bulk** (17–18): the Boltzmann distribution
  over a ladder as the temperature changes, the partition function as the
  area of that picture, heat capacities rising as modes switch on.
- **Thermodynamics as paths** (19–23): a PV plane where the reader drags a
  state through isothermal and adiabatic paths and the work is the shaded
  area; a Carnot cycle; phase diagrams with a moving state point.
- **Molecules in motion** (27–30): the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution with
  a gas box at the same temperature; collision theory; a potential energy
  surface in 3D with a trajectory rolling over the saddle.

The thread through the book, which the figures can make visible, is that
**every bulk property is an average over quantum levels**: the ladder of
Chapter 1 returns in Chapter 17 populated by a Boltzmann factor. The same
level diagram drawn in the same style throughout would let a reader see
that.
