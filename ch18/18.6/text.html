Summing the rotational levels

The rotational energy levels of a diatomic molecule are given by:

$$ \kErot(J) = \kBt J (J + 1) \tag{18.6.1} $$

where:

$$ \kBt = \dfrac{h}{8 π^2 \kI c} $$

Here, $\kBt$ is the rotational constant expressed in cm-1. The rotational energy levels are given by:

$$ \kEj = \dfrac{J(J+1) h^2}{8 \pi \kI} $$

where $\kI$ is the moment of inertia of the molecule given by $\kmu \kr^2$ for a diatomic, and $\kmu$ is the reduced mass, and $\kr$ the bond length (assuming rigid rotor approximation). The energies can be also expressed in terms of the rotational temperature, $\kThetarot$, defined as:

$$ \kThetarot = \dfrac{r^2}{8 \pi^2 \kI k} \tag{18.6.2} $$

The interpretation of $\kThetarot$ is as an estimate of the temperature at which thermal energy ($\approx k\kT$) is comparable to the spacing between rotational energy levels. At about this temperature the population of excited rotational levels becomes important. See Table 1.

In the summation for the expression for rotational partition function ($\kqrot$), Equation 18.6.3, we can do an explicit summation:

$$ \kqrot = \sum_{j=0} (2J+1) e^{-\kEJ/ k \kT} \tag{18.6.3} $$

if only a finite number of terms contribute. The factor $(2J+1)$ for each term in the expansion accounts for the degeneracy of a rotational state $J$. For each allowed energy $\kEJ$ from Equation 18.6.1 there are $(2 J + 1)$ eigenstates. The Boltzmann factor must be multiplied by $(2J+ 1)$ to properly account for the degeneracy these states:

$$ (2J+ 1)e^{ -\kEJ / k \kT} $$

Correcting for symmetry

For a homonuclear diatomic molecule, rotating the molecule by 180° brings the molecule into a configuration which is indistinguishable from the original configuration. This leads to an overcounting of the accessible states. To correct for this, we divide the partition function by $σ$, which is called the symmetry number and is equal to the distinct number of ways by which a molecule can be brought into identical configurations by rotations. The rotational partition function becomes:

$$ \kqrot= \dfrac{k\kT}{\kBt σ} \tag{18.6.5} $$

or commonly expressed in terms of $\kThetarot$:

$$ \kqrot= \dfrac{\kT}{ \kThetarot σ} \tag{18.6.6} $$

Example 18.6.1

What is the rotational partition function of $H_2$ at 300 K?

Solution

The value of $\kBt$ for $H_2$ is 60.864 cm-1. The value of $k \kT$ in cm-1 can be obtained by dividing it by $hc$, i.e., which is $k\kT/hc = 209.7\; cm^{-1}$ at 300 K. $σ = 2$ for a homonuclear molecule. Therefore from Equation 18.6.5,

$$ \begin{aligned} \kqrot &= \dfrac{k\kT}{\kBt σ} \\[4pt] &= \dfrac{209.7 \;cm^{-1} }{(2) (60.864\; cm^{-1})} \\[4pt] &= 1.723 \end{aligned} $$

Since the rotational frequency of $H_2$ is quite large, only the first few rotational states are accessible at 300 K
