Products minus reactants

Entropy is a state function, so we can calculate values for a process using any path. This allows us to calculate the entropy change of a chemical reaction using standard entropies. Specifically, we sum the entropies of the products and subtract the entropies of the reactions:

$$ \Delta_{rxn}S^\circ = \sum_{\text{Products}}{v_i S^\circ_i} - \sum_{\text{Reactants}}{v_i S^\circ_i} $$

Where $v_i$ is the stoichiometric coefficient. Let's look at the combustion of methane:

$$ \mathrm{CH_4} \left( g \right) + 2 \mathrm{O_2} \left( g \right) \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{H_2O} + \mathrm{CO_2}\left(g\right) $$

The standard entropies are:

Table
MoleculeEntropy $\left(\frac{\text{J}}{\text{mol}\cdot\text{K}}\right)$
$\mathrm{CH_4}$$186.25^1$
$\mathrm{O_2}$$205.15^1$
$\mathrm{H_2O}$$188.84^1$
$\mathrm{CO_2}$$213.79^1$

The entropy for the combustion of methane is:

$$ \Delta S^\circ = \left[ 2 \left( 188.84 \right) + 1 \left( 213.70 \right) \right] - \left[ 1 \left( 186.25 \right) + 2 \left( 205.15 \right) \right] = -5.17 \: \frac{\text{J}}{\text{mol} \cdot \text{K}} $$
