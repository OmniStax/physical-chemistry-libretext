$$ d\kU \le \kT d\kSent - \kP d\kV $$

This equality expresses $\kU$ as a function of two variables, entropy and volume: $\kU(\kSent,\kV)$. $\kSent$ and $\kV$ are the natural variables of $\kU$.

Entropy and heat capacity

If we know how $\kCV$ changes with temperature, we can calculate the change in entropy, $\Delta \kSent$. Since heat capacity is always a positive value, entropy must increase as the temperature increases. There is nothing to stop us from expressing $\kU$ in other variables, e.g. $\kT$ and $\kV$.
