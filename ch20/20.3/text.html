Circular integrals

Because entropy is a state function, it integrates to zero over any circular path going back to initial conditions, just like $\kU$ and $\kHenth$:

$$ \oint d\kSent =0 $$ $$ \oint d\kHenth =0 $$ $$ \oint d\kU =0 $$

As discussed previously, we can use this fact to revisit the isotherm + isochore + adiabat circular path (Figure 20.3.1).

Along adiabat B and isochore C:

  • There is no heat transfer along adiabat B:
$$ \kqrevB = 0 $$
  • There is no work along isochore C:
$$ \delta \kw=0 $$
  • But the temperature changes from $\kTtwo$ back to $\kTone$. This requires heat:
$$ \kqrevC=\kCV\Delta \kT $$

Along the isotherm A:

  • We have seen that
$$ \kqrevA = \knmol R\kT \ln \dfrac{\kVb}{\kVa} $$

The quantities qrev,A, qrev,B, and qrev,C are not the same, which once again underlines that heat is a path function. How about entropy?

Entropy along the two paths

First, consider the combined paths of B and C:

$$ \kqrevBC = \int _{\kTtwo}^{\kTone} \kCv d\kT $$ $$ \int d\kSBC = \int \dfrac{d\kqrevBC}{\kT} = \int _{\kTtwo}^{\kTone} \dfrac{\kCv}{\kT} d\kT $$

We had seen this integral before from Section 19-6, albeit from $\kTone$ to $\kTtwo$:

$$ \Delta \kSBC = \knmol R\ln \dfrac{\kVb}{\kVa} \tag{20.3.1} $$

(Notice sign in Equation 20.3.1 is positive)

Along the isotherm A:

$$ \kqrevA = \knmol R\kT \ln\frac{\kVb}{\kVa} $$

$\kT$ is a constant so we can just divide $q_{rec,A}$ by $\kT$ to get $\Delta \kSA$:

$$ \Delta \kSA = \knmol R\ln \dfrac{\kVb}{\kVa} $$

We took two different paths to get start and end at the same points. Both paths had the same change in entropy. Clearly entropy is a state function while $\kqrev$ is not.
